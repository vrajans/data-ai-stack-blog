Every data platform eventually gets The Email: the bill doubled, nobody knows why, and finance wants a plan by Friday. Teams respond with a cleanup sprint — pause some warehouses, delete some tables — and six months later the bill is back where it was.

Cost doesn't stay down through one-off cleanups. It stays down when it's treated as a **property of the architecture**: visible per team and product, measured against the value delivered, and controlled by defaults and guardrails rather than heroics.

This is Part 10, the final part of the [Data Architecture series](/series/data-architecture). It pulls the cost threads from every earlier part — storage layout, catalogs, modeling, streaming, platforms and AI — into one practical playbook.

<Callout type="key" title="The short answer">
**Measure cost per unit of value, not just the total.** Normalize every bill (the FOCUS standard helps), allocate it to owning teams with enforced tags, and divide by the work delivered — dashboard queries, pipeline runs, AI answers. Then pull the levers that matter most: **avoid scanning data you don't need, process incrementally, right-size and auto-suspend compute, isolate workloads, keep compute next to the data, and give every team and agent a budget**. Performance and cost usually improve together: the cheapest query is the one that reads the least data.
</Callout>

## Where the money goes

A data platform bill has five parts. Their proportions vary, but the pattern is consistent: compute dominates, storage is cheap but grows quietly, data movement surprises, AI is the fastest-growing line, and tools and people are often left out of the picture entirely.

<CostAnatomy />

Two things follow from this. First, **most savings come from compute**, and most compute waste comes from reading more data than necessary, recomputing things that didn't change, and paying for capacity nobody is using. Second, **storage savings are real but smaller** — worth doing with lifecycle rules and retention policies, but rarely where the big wins are.

## Know what your pricing model rewards

Each platform's pricing model rewards different behavior. Optimizing without knowing which one you're on is guesswork:

| Pricing model | Where you'll see it | What drives the bill | What to tune first |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bytes scanned** | BigQuery on-demand ($6.25 per TiB, US list price), Athena | Data read per query | Partitioning, clustering, selecting fewer columns, maximum-bytes limits |
| **Slots or capacity units** | BigQuery editions ($0.04–$0.10 per slot-hour, US), Microsoft Fabric F-SKUs | Reserved or autoscaled capacity over time | Workload isolation, autoscaling limits, scheduling heavy jobs off-peak |
| **Credits per running second** | Snowflake warehouses (per-second billing, 60-second minimum per resume) | Warehouse size × time running | Auto-suspend, right-sizing, separate warehouses per workload |
| **DBUs plus cloud infrastructure** | Databricks (classic compute bills cloud VMs separately) | Cluster size × runtime, plus VMs and storage | Serverless vs classic per workload, autoscaling, job clusters instead of all-purpose |
| **Per service, pay-as-you-go** | AWS native (Glue, EMR, Redshift Serverless, S3) | Many small meters | Tagging and per-service dashboards, then the largest meters |
| **Tokens and requests** | LLM and embedding APIs, vector search | Tokens per answer × answers | Caching, model routing, shorter context, batch jobs |

Capacity models need extra care: on Microsoft Fabric, overage billing for capacity is enabled by default on new capacities and charged at a premium to pay-as-you-go rates, so one runaway notebook can raise a bill nobody planned for.

## FinOps for data: Inform, Optimize, Operate

The FinOps Foundation's framework describes three phases that map well onto data platforms:

- **Inform** — make cost visible: who spends what, on which product, and what it achieves.
- **Optimize** — act on the biggest opportunities: technical levers and pricing commitments.
- **Operate** — make it routine: budgets, guardrails, reviews and ownership.

The practice has grown well beyond cloud infrastructure. The FinOps Foundation's *State of FinOps 2026* survey (1,192 respondents, February 2026) found that **98% of FinOps teams now manage AI spend**, up from 31% two years earlier — and named FinOps for AI and "shifting left" (cost awareness during design, not after the bill) as top priorities.

### FOCUS: one format for every bill

The **FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification (FOCUS)** defines a common schema for billing data — columns such as `BilledCost`, `EffectiveCost`, `ListCost`, `ServiceName`, `ChargePeriodStart` and `Tags` — so you can analyze AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Databricks, Snowflake and SaaS bills with the same queries. FOCUS 1.4 was ratified in June 2026. AWS publishes a generally available FOCUS export; Azure, Google Cloud, Databricks and Snowflake offer FOCUS exports in preview. FOCUS 1.5, scheduled for ratification in December 2026, adds AI model and token columns and requester attribution — useful for tracking cost per agent.

Load these exports into your lakehouse as a **cost data product** with an owner, like any other dataset. Then the usual tools — SQL, dashboards, alerts — work on cost too.

## Unit economics: the number that changes behavior

A total bill tells you *that* spending grew. A unit cost tells you *whether that's a problem*. If the bill grew 40% while dashboard queries grew 80%, cost per query fell — that's success, not a crisis.

<UnitCostFlow />

Good unit metrics for data platforms:

- **Cost per dashboard query** or per active BI user — for analytics.
- **Cost per pipeline run** and per million rows processed — for data engineering.
- **Cost per data product per month** — for domain ownership (Part 7).
- **Cost per AI answer** or per agent task — for AI workloads (Part 9).
- **Storage cost per TB of useful data** — to spot duplication and retention bloat.

The data for the denominator comes from your own logs: query history, orchestrator run logs, application metrics. Join it to allocated cost by product tag, and publish the trend weekly to the owning teams.

### Try it: showback, unit cost and anomalies in SQL

This runs in [DuckDB](https://duckdb.org/) (version 1.1 or later) with generated sample data in FOCUS-style columns. Replace the two `CREATE TABLE` statements with your real FOCUS export and usage logs, and the queries stay the same.

```sql
-- focus_costs.sql  →  run with:  duckdb < focus_costs.sql   (DuckDB 1.1+)

-- 1) Two weeks of daily costs in FOCUS-style columns (a real export has many more)
CREATE TABLE focus_costs AS
SELECT g.d::DATE                                   AS ChargePeriodStart,
       r.ServiceName,
       r.ServiceCategory,
       r.ResourceId,
       r.Tags,
       round(r.daily * CASE WHEN r.ResourceId = 'wh-adhoc' AND g.d >= DATE '2026-11-09'
                            THEN 2.6 ELSE 1 END, 2) AS EffectiveCost
FROM generate_series(DATE '2026-11-02', DATE '2026-11-15', INTERVAL 1 DAY) AS g(d)
CROSS JOIN (VALUES
  ('Snowflake',  'Analytics',               'wh-bi',         '{"team":"finance","product":"revenue-dashboards"}', 42.00),
  ('Snowflake',  'Analytics',               'wh-adhoc',      '{"team":"data-science"}',                           18.00),
  ('Databricks', 'Compute',                 'job-orders',    '{"team":"commerce","product":"orders-pipeline"}',   25.00),
  ('Amazon S3',  'Storage',                 'lake-bucket',   '{}',                                                 9.50),
  ('Model API',  'AI and Machine Learning', 'support-llm',   '{"team":"support","product":"support-assistant"}', 12.00)
) AS r(ServiceName, ServiceCategory, ResourceId, Tags, daily);

-- 2) Usage from your own logs: how many units each product delivered per day
CREATE TABLE product_usage AS
SELECT g.d::DATE AS day, u.product, u.unit, u.qty
FROM generate_series(DATE '2026-11-02', DATE '2026-11-15', INTERVAL 1 DAY) AS g(d)
CROSS JOIN (VALUES
  ('revenue-dashboards', 'dashboard query', 1400),
  ('orders-pipeline',    'pipeline run',      24),
  ('support-assistant',  'AI answer',        900)
) AS u(product, unit, qty);

-- 3) Showback: last 7 days of cost by team, including spend nobody owns
SELECT coalesce(json_extract_string(Tags, '$.team'), '(untagged)') AS team,
       round(sum(EffectiveCost), 2)                                AS cost_7d
FROM focus_costs
WHERE ChargePeriodStart >= DATE '2026-11-09'
GROUP BY ALL
ORDER BY cost_7d DESC;

-- 4) Unit economics: what one dashboard query, pipeline run or AI answer costs
SELECT u.product,
       u.unit,
       round(sum(c.EffectiveCost), 2)                    AS cost_7d,
       sum(u.qty)                                        AS units_7d,
       round(sum(c.EffectiveCost) / sum(u.qty), 4)       AS cost_per_unit
FROM product_usage AS u
JOIN focus_costs AS c
  ON json_extract_string(c.Tags, '$.product') = u.product
 AND c.ChargePeriodStart = u.day
WHERE u.day >= DATE '2026-11-09'
GROUP BY ALL
ORDER BY cost_per_unit DESC;

-- 5) Anomalies: resources whose cost grew more than 30% week over week
WITH weekly AS (
  SELECT ResourceId,
         sum(EffectiveCost) FILTER (WHERE ChargePeriodStart <  DATE '2026-11-09') AS prev_week,
         sum(EffectiveCost) FILTER (WHERE ChargePeriodStart >= DATE '2026-11-09') AS this_week
  FROM focus_costs
  GROUP BY ResourceId
)
SELECT ResourceId,
       round(prev_week, 2) AS prev_week,
       round(this_week, 2) AS this_week,
       round(100 * (this_week - prev_week) / prev_week, 1) AS change_pct
FROM weekly
WHERE this_week > prev_week * 1.3
ORDER BY change_pct DESC;
```

What the three answers tell you on this sample data:

- **Showback:** data science is the biggest spender this week ($327.60), and $66.50 of storage has no owner at all — the first thing to fix is the tag.
- **Unit cost:** a revenue-dashboard query costs about 3 cents, a support-assistant answer about 1.3 cents, and an orders pipeline run about $1.04. Those are the numbers to track week over week.
- **Anomaly:** the ad-hoc warehouse grew **160%** week over week ($126 → $327.60) — typical of a new heavy notebook, a missing auto-suspend or a query without filters.

## The levers that actually move cost and performance

Most of these were covered in depth earlier in the series. Here they are as one checklist, in rough order of impact:

| Lever | Typical waste | Fix | More in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Read less data** | `SELECT *`, missing filters, full scans of large tables | Partitioning and clustering, column pruning, filters on partition columns, bytes-scanned limits | [Part 3](/blog/open-table-formats-deep-dive) |
| **Keep tables healthy** | Thousands of small files, stale snapshots, bloated metadata | Compaction, snapshot expiry, orphan-file cleanup — or managed maintenance | [Part 4](/blog/lakehouse-catalogs-metadata-control-plane) |
| **Process incrementally** | Full refreshes of tables where 1% changed | Incremental models, merge on keys, change data feeds | [Part 5](/blog/data-modeling-modern-stack) |
| **Right-size latency** | Streaming jobs running 24/7 for data read once a day | Batch or micro-batch unless real-time pays off | [Part 6](/blog/batch-streaming-real-time-architectures) |
| **Right-size compute** | Oversized warehouses and clusters, no auto-suspend | Start small, scale on evidence, short auto-suspend, job clusters | [Part 8](/blog/reference-architectures-aws-azure-gcp-databricks-snowflake) |
| **Isolate workloads** | One shared warehouse where ad-hoc exploration slows dashboards | Separate compute per workload class, each with its own budget | This part |
| **Precompute what's reused** | Fifty dashboards recomputing the same aggregation | Aggregate tables, materialized views, result caching | [Part 5](/blog/data-modeling-modern-stack) |
| **Keep compute near data** | Cross-region and cross-cloud reads, copies between platforms | One home region per domain, sharing instead of copying | [Part 8](/blog/reference-architectures-aws-azure-gcp-databricks-snowflake) |
| **Tier and expire storage** | Raw data kept forever on hot storage, long time-travel windows | Lifecycle tiers, retention by data class, shorter time travel on non-critical tables | [Part 2](/blog/warehouse-vs-lake-vs-lakehouse) |
| **Control dev and test** | Full production copies in every environment, clusters left running overnight | Zero-copy clones or samples, schedules and auto-termination | This part |

<Callout type="tip" title="Performance and cost usually move together">
On almost every modern engine, a query that reads less data is both faster and cheaper. Fixing the top 20 queries or jobs by cost each month — usually a small fraction of all workloads — typically delivers most of the available savings, and makes dashboards faster at the same time.
</Callout>

## AI costs: a new line with its own levers

AI workloads add costs that don't behave like queries: tokens per answer, embedding runs over whole document collections, re-embedding when models change, vector index storage and serving, and GPU time. The levers:

- **Measure cost per answer or per task**, not just the monthly API bill — with tags per product and, as FOCUS 1.5 lands, per model and per agent.
- **Cache** repeated questions and retrieval results.
- **Route by difficulty:** a small, cheap model handles routine requests; a larger model only when needed.
- **Trim context:** better retrieval (Part 9) means fewer, more relevant chunks — cheaper *and* more accurate.
- **Batch** offline work such as embeddings and summarization, where lower-priority pricing is often available.
- **Give every agent a budget** and hard limits on calls per task, so a looping agent stops before the bill grows.

## Guardrails: make the cheap path the default

People don't read cost policies; they use defaults. Build the policy into the platform:

- **Tags at creation time.** Refuse to create warehouses, clusters, buckets or jobs without `team`, `product` and `environment` tags.
- **Budgets and alerts per team** — Snowflake budgets and resource monitors, Databricks budget policies and system billing tables, BigQuery quotas and maximum bytes billed, cloud budgets — with alerts to the owning team, not only to finance.
- **Safe defaults:** short auto-suspend, query timeouts, small default sizes, auto-termination for development clusters.
- **Approval for big commitments:** reserved capacity and committed-use discounts only after usage is stable and measured.
- **Showback first, chargeback later.** Start by showing teams their costs; move to charging them only when the allocation is trusted.
- **A monthly cost review** of the top spenders and the biggest unit-cost changes, with owners present.

## Committed-use discounts: last, not first

Every platform offers discounts for commitment — reserved capacity, committed-use discounts, prepaid credits. They're worth having, but **commit after optimizing**, not before. Committing to today's wasteful baseline locks in the waste. Optimize, watch usage stabilize for a month or two, then commit to the steady floor and keep the peaks on demand.

## A 30/60/90-day plan

1. **Days 1–30 — Inform.** Turn on FOCUS (or native) cost exports for every platform, load them into a cost table, enforce tags on new resources, and publish a weekly showback by team. Find the untagged spend.
2. **Days 31–60 — Optimize.** Fix the top 20 queries and jobs, set auto-suspend and right-size compute, separate the noisiest workload, add lifecycle and retention rules, and define three unit-cost metrics with their owners.
3. **Days 61–90 — Operate.** Budgets and alerts for every team and AI product, guardrails as code, a monthly review, and — once usage is stable — the first commitment purchase.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Cleanup sprints instead of defaults** — savings that disappear within a quarter.
- **Total-bill targets with no unit metrics**, which punish growth and reward doing less.
- **Finance-only dashboards** that engineers never see.
- **Committing to capacity before optimizing.**
- **One giant shared warehouse** where nobody owns the cost.
- **Ignoring data movement and AI lines** because they're "small" — they're the fastest-growing.

## Key takeaways

- Cost is an **architecture property**: design for visibility, ownership and efficient defaults from day one.
- **Compute dominates**; most waste is reading unnecessary data, recomputing unchanged data and paying for idle capacity.
- Know **what your pricing model rewards** — bytes scanned, slots, running seconds, DBUs or tokens — and tune for that.
- Normalize bills with **FOCUS**, allocate with **enforced tags**, and track **unit costs** per query, pipeline run, data product and AI answer.
- **Performance and cost improve together** when queries read less data.
- **Guardrails and budgets beat cleanup sprints**; commitments come *after* optimization.
